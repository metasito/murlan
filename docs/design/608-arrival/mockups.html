How a traded card arrives

#532 asked how both cards move at once and mocked three answers. You chose none of them and recorded the requirement instead. #533 shipped the flight anyway, against a question that was still open — so this is that question, asked again about the half nobody drew: not the journey, the landing.

let the cards flow manually in each player's deck, like taking its spot, not a sudden animation — the owner, playing #533

I want it to be as clear as possible for all the players involved not only the 2 involved in the exchange — the owner, #532's decision comment, 2026-08-29

Those two sentences are the whole brief and they pull in different directions. "Taking its spot" is a quiet, local thing inside one hand. "Clear for all the players" has to carry across the table to somebody whose own cards are a fan of backs 24px wide. Each option leans on one of them.

These are the real table. The felt and its lamp, the seat rings, the card faces, the fans of backs and the hand's own arc are taken verbatim from tests/e2e/fixtures/prototype-table.html, the prototype the shipped table is built from — same 844×390 phone, same radius 2200 × s arc, same cardW × .68 step. So the overlap you see is the overlap a card actually has to travel behind.

Every demo is the same moment from the same seat: you are the bottom hand, Bea is the top seat, and the card she gave you is landing at its sorted place among your eight. Cesk, on the left, has no part in the trade and is watching. Press replay to run it again.

Option A

The room is made first

The hand opens a gap at the card's sorted index before the card gets there, and the card descends into a space already waiting. Two beats, and the first of them is the hand answering rather than the card arriving.

What you see. Your own cards part, and only then does the new one come down. Because the gap opens first, your eye is already at the index when the card reaches it — you are told where before you are told what.

What Cesk sees. Your fan gets one wider, in two steps. The parting is the larger of the two movements and it happens on an otherwise still table, so it is the beat that carries from across the felt.

Against it: the longest of the three, and the gap is briefly a hole in a hand nobody has put a card in — which reads as a missing card for as long as it is empty.

Reduced
Motion.reduced.shift = 0 collapses both beats: the row is already re-stepped and the card already in it. One frame, no hole.
Numbers
computeHandLayout(n+1), plus GAP_CARDS and gapShift() — all three in hand.tsx today.
Two places
Yes, for one beat. The card is in the flight layer while the row is already stepping for it.

Option B

It lands at the edge and walks in

The card joins the hand at the near end, as a real member of the row, and then travels along it to its sorted place while the cards it passes step aside. Nothing is ever in two places: the flight ends the moment the card is part of the fan.

What you see. The most literal reading of "taking its spot": the card is handed to you, and then it finds where it belongs. It is also the only option that shows the sort, which is a rule nobody is ever told.

What Cesk sees. The clearest of the three by some distance — the motion continues inside your fan after the flight has stopped, so there is a second, separate event to notice. A watcher who missed the flight still catches this.

Against it: watch how much of the card is behind its neighbours for most of the walk. At eight cards the arc is open and it reads; a real hand of thirteen compresses to MIN_READABLE_STEP against a 64px card, and the travelling card is then more than half hidden the whole way.

Reduced
The walk is the whole option, so it becomes its endpoint: card in place, row re-stepped. It degrades to A's still frame — that is, to nothing of its own.
Numbers
computeHandLayout(n+1) only, held for the whole sequence. No gap maths at all.
Two places
No. The only option where the card is a row member from the first frame it is in the hand.

Option C

The fan parts around it as it lands

One beat, not two. The card flies to the coordinate it will occupy and the neighbours move out of its way during the arrival rather than before it, so the row is never open and never jumps. This is the shape #531 already built for a dragged card being released, pointed at an incoming one.

What you see. The hand absorbs the card. No hole and no second beat, which makes it the calmest and the shortest — one continuous movement from the felt into the fan.

What Cesk sees. The fan breathes once, at the same instant the flight ends. One event rather than two, so there is less to catch — but it is simultaneous with the thing he was already looking at.

Against it: the parting is at its smallest exactly when the card is at its most distracting, so from across the table the index is the least legible of the three. It serves the receiver over the audience.

Reduced
Already one beat, so Motion.reduced.shift = 0 is genuinely the same picture with the travel removed. The best degradation of the three.
Numbers
computeHandLayout(n+1), and the release path's settleX/settleY/settleRot + landing, which already land a card "as the fan closes around it".
Two places
Momentarily. As A, but the overlap is the arrival itself rather than an extra beat before it.

What to weigh: none of the three needs a number that is not already in components/handLayout.ts or components/table/hand.tsx — #531's drag-to-reorder built the gap and the landing, and this is that machinery pointed at a card arriving from the felt instead of from a finger. So cost is not the tiebreak. B is the only one that survives being watched, because it puts a second, separate event after the flight — which is exactly the failure #532 recorded against set 3 the first time. C is the best thing to receive, and the only one whose reduced-motion form is still recognisably itself. A is the compromise and carries the one thing neither of the others does: a hole, which is a card missing until it is not. B and C are combinable — land at the edge (B's second event, for the audience) and let the fan part around the card as it walks (C's continuous absorption, for the receiver). That is a fourth answer, and it is the one to ask for if neither column wins on its own.

What this does not decide

The flight itself. Set 3's question — one lane, two lanes, meeting in the middle — is still open and still unchosen; #533 shipped two lanes without an answer. All three options here begin the moment the flight ends, so any of them sits under any flight.

What is already in the repo

  • gapAt / gapShift() / GAP_CARDS — opens a card-wide gap at an index and splits the row either side of it, so the hand's centre holds still.
  • settleX / settleY / settleRot / landing — flies a card to a slot "as the fan closes around it", on motionMs("shift").
  • computeHandLayout(n, room, cardW, availW) — the row's step and span, which every slot coordinate comes from.
