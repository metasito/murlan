Everything is derived from the short edge

A landscape phone is short of height, never width, so height is the only budget worth writing down. One number governs the table: --s, the device's short edge over 390. An iPhone SE runs at 0.82, a 17 Pro Max at 1.13, and every card, chip, gap and radius is a multiple of it. That is #100 in a line — no breakpoints, no tablet branch, no 500px of empty felt.

Three scales sit on top of it. The field runs at 0.92, your hand at 0.78, the opponents' backs at 0.60 — measured as a share of screen height, that is 21%, 18% and 6%. Magic Arena tilts its board so the player's half reads about 60/40 against the opponent's; we can't tilt a camera, so scale does the same job.

Two sizes are not aesthetic choices

The hand steps 34px, set by the thumb. Tighter reads calmer, and I tried it — but each buried card's tappable strip drops under the 44pt floor, so the fan is fitted to the target rather than the other way round. Gioca and Passa are 56pt, down from 64: still clear of the floor, and they stopped anchoring the corners like furniture.

The rest is a width budget, which is what was actually missing. Every arc gets a share of the table instead of all the room it can reach — the hand is pinned at 54% and never stretches, and the field takes what the seats leave it. A tris occupies 15% of the width; a thirteen-card A→K run compresses to 45% and stops. Same layout, no special case, and every rank still readable because the arcs solve for the index corner — the one part of a card guaranteed to survive the overlap.

The cutout is a control rail

The old rule was to treat the notch as darkness and stay out of it, which cost 67px at the top of every screen — including the phones that have no cutout at all. The better rule is the one you gave me: a cutout can never sit on a card, but it sits happily between two controls. So the column it occupies became the rail — menu at the top, reactions at the bottom, cutout in the gap.

The rail is max(52 × --s, inset + 12), so it widens for a Dynamic Island and holds its floor on an iPhone 8. Switch handsets in the picker and watch: the cutout appears and disappears, and nothing moves. That is the test — a layout that shifts when the notch toggles was never sized for it.

Focus mode

Open the menu and turn it on. Chips, counts, names and the reactions button all leave; the cards, the avatars and the menu stay. What carries the turn afterwards is the table itself — the lamp and the seat ring, not a label. Card backs and felt colour are deliberately absent from this sheet: those belong to the main menu, before a hand starts, not mid-manche.

What ships

Four sized ready-for-agent tickets under #98 and #100: replace WEB_TOP_PAD / WEB_BOTTOM_PAD with real insets and add viewport-fit=cover; derive --s from the short edge; build the rail and move the menu and reactions into it; add the settings sheet with focus mode and the exit confirmation. One caution carried over from #94: CardBacks reuses the felt palette as its own field, so any change to the default felt makes the backs vanish — that dependency gets broken first or it takes the rest with it.
